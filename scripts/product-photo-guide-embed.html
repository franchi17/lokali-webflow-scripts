What works
Photos that help products sell
Shoppers buy what they can picture clearly. You don't need a studio, just good light and a clean shot.
Great
Clean background
Product on a plain surface: white, wood, or a single soft color. Nothing competing for attention.
Great
In real life
Show it being used or styled: a candle lit, a bag worn, a treat on a plate. People picture owning it.
Great
A few angles
Every plan lets you add more than one photo: the front, a side, and a close-up of the detail. Free has room for 2 per listing, Pro 5, Featured 10. The extra shots are what turn a maybe into a yes.
Skip this
Cluttered scenes
Too much going on and shoppers can't tell what's for sale. Clear the table first.
Skip this
Dark or blurry
If buyers can't see it clearly, they scroll past. Move to a window and tap to focus.
Skip this
Heavy filters & watermarks
Big logos, stamps, or strong filters make the real colors hard to trust. Keep it true to life.
Quick tips
Four habits for better product shots
None of these require anything you don't already have.
1
Use natural light
Set up near a window during the day, with the light coming from the side. It makes colors look true and shows off texture. No flash needed.
2
Fill the frame
Get close so the product is the star. A little space around the edges is good, but you shouldn't have to squint to find it.
3
Center for the square crop
Your cover photo can show as a square in some places. Keep the product centered so nothing important sits right at the edge.
4
Keep your set consistent
Same background and lighting across your products makes your whole shop look polished and pulled-together, like a real storefront.
